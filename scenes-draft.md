# Second Take: Scene Drafts

Mood deltas are signed. Trigger labels match the partner's lists exactly.

## SCENE 1: The raise conversation

- **id:** raise-conversation
- **Category:** Work | **Difficulty:** medium | **Duration:** 3 min
- **Situation:** You've been at the company a year. When your teammate Priya left, you took over her reporting work and still shipped the billing migration. Your pay hasn't changed. You booked 20 minutes with your manager.
- **Your goal:** Ask for a specific raise and leave with a clear next step, not a "we'll see."

### Partner
- **Name / age / role:** Marcus Lee, 45, Engineering Director
- **One-line description:** Fair, blunt, careful with budget.
- **Secretly wants:** To keep you. He has a small budget for exceptions, but he needs strong evidence to argue your case with HR.
- **Softens when:** You bring specific numbers | You name a clear figure | You're flexible on timing
- **Digs in when:** You're vague about why | You use another offer as a threat | You apologise for asking
- **Verbal habit:** "Walk me through it."

### Rubric
| id | Skill | Description | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| evidence | Backed it with impact | Gave concrete results, not effort | Lead with one result he can repeat to someone else, with a number in it. |
| ask | Named a clear ask | Said a specific figure | Say the number out loud, early. A range invites the bottom of it. |
| confidence | Asked without apologising | No hedging, threats or self-shrinking | Drop "sorry" and "I know it's a bad time." Just say why you're here. |
| nextstep | Left with a next step | Ended with a task and a date | Before you leave, ask what he needs from you and by when. |

### Turn 1
- **Guarded:** "Sure. I've got about ten minutes, actually. What's up?"
- **Neutral:** "Sure, I've got twenty minutes. What's up?"
- **Open:** "Hey, good timing. What's on your mind?"

**A (strong):** "I want to talk about my pay. This year I took on Priya's work and shipped the billing migration, and I think my salary should reflect that."
- Reaction: "Okay. That's fair to bring up. Walk me through it."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: evidence +1, confidence +1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: You named the topic and the reason in one breath, with no apology.

**B (subtle):** "So, um, this might be awkward, but is there any room to revisit my compensation at some point?"
- Reaction: "At some point? Sure, I guess. What are you thinking?"
- Mood: 0 | Skills: confidence -1, ask -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You apologise for asking"
- Coach note: Polite, but "might be awkward" and "at some point" tell him this can wait.
- Better line: "I'd like to talk about my pay. My role has grown a lot this year."

**C (backfire):** "A recruiter reached out with a much better offer, so I think we need to talk about my salary."
- Reaction: "Oh. Okay. Are you planning to leave?"
- Mood: -2 | Skills: evidence -1, confidence -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You use another offer as a threat"
- Coach note: Opening with another offer turns a raise talk into a retention scare. Now he's defending, not listening.
- Better line: "I want to talk about my pay. This year I took on Priya's work and shipped the billing migration."

### Turn 2
- **Guarded:** "Look, before we go further, is this about another offer? Because that changes things."
- **Neutral:** "Okay. Walk me through it. What's changed in your role?"
- **Open:** "Yeah, the migration was a big deal. Walk me through the rest."

**A (strong):** "When Priya left, I picked up the reporting pipeline on top of my work. The migration shipped two weeks early and cut failed payments by about 18%."
- Reaction: "Eighteen percent. I didn't know it was that high."
- Mood: +2 | Skills: evidence +1 | Trigger: soften, "You bring specific numbers"
- Coach note: One concrete result with a number gives him something he can repeat to HR.

**B (subtle):** "Honestly, I've just been working really hard. Staying late, taking on whatever comes up."
- Reaction: "I know you work hard. Everyone here does, though."
- Mood: -1 | Skills: evidence -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You're vague about why"
- Coach note: Effort is real, but it's hard for him to argue for. Results travel further than hours.
- Better line: "I picked up the reporting pipeline, and the migration cut failed payments by about 18%."

**C (backfire):** "I mean, you've seen my work. I shouldn't have to spell it out."
- Reaction: "I'd actually like you to. That's what I'll have to do for HR."
- Mood: -2 | Skills: evidence -1, confidence -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You're vague about why"
- Coach note: It sounds confident, but it hands him the job of building your case.
- Better line: "Sure. The migration shipped two weeks early and cut failed payments by about 18%."

### Turn 3
- **Guarded:** "Alright. Even if I agree, the review cycle's in March. I can't do much before that."
- **Neutral:** "Okay, that's solid. What are you asking for, exactly?"
- **Open:** "That's a strong year. So what number did you have in mind?"

**A (strong):** "I'm asking for a 15% raise. That brings me in line with what this role pays elsewhere, and I've got the data if it helps."
- Reaction: "Fifteen. Okay. That's specific. I appreciate that."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: ask +1, confidence +1 | Trigger: soften, "You name a clear figure"
- Coach note: A clear number with a reason behind it is easy for him to take upstairs.

**B (subtle):** "Whatever you think is fair, honestly. I trust you."
- Reaction: "That's kind, but it doesn't really help me make the case."
- Mood: 0 | Skills: ask -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You're vague about why"
- Coach note: Leaving the number to him feels generous, but he'll anchor low to stay safe on budget.
- Better line: "I'm asking for 15%. That's in line with what this role pays elsewhere."

**C (backfire):** "Sorry, I know budgets are tight. Maybe something small, if that's even possible?"
- Reaction: "Something small. Okay. I'll see what I can do."
- Mood: -1 | Skills: ask -1, confidence -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You apologise for asking"
- Coach note: You negotiated against yourself before he said a word.
- Better line: "I'm asking for 15%, and I've got the market data if it helps."

### Turn 4
- **Guarded:** "I hear you. I'm just not sure I can promise anything right now."
- **Neutral:** "I can't approve it myself, but I can take it to HR. Timing's the hard part."
- **Open:** "I think I can make a case for this. Timing's the only tricky bit."

**A (strong):** "That's fair. What would you need from me to make the case, and could we aim for a decision before March?"
- Reaction: "Send me a one-pager with those numbers by Friday. I'll push for February."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: nextstep +1, confidence +1 | Trigger: soften, "You're flexible on timing"
- Coach note: You gave a little on timing, kept the ask, and left with a task and a date.

**B (subtle):** "Okay, no worries. Just let me know whenever you hear something."
- Reaction: "Will do."
- Mood: 0 | Skills: nextstep -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Friendly, but "whenever" hands the timeline to a busy person. It may quietly drift.
- Better line: "What would you need from me to make the case, and by when?"

**C (backfire):** "If it can't happen before March, I might need to look at other options."
- Reaction: "Then I guess you'll have to do what's right for you."
- Mood: -2 | Skills: nextstep -1, confidence -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You use another offer as a threat"
- Coach note: An ultimatum at the end undoes the case you built and puts him on the defensive.
- Better line: "That's fair. What do you need from me, and could we aim for before March?"

### Endings
- **Guarded:** "Let me think about it. I'll get back to you."
- **Neutral:** "Send me what you've got. I'll raise it with HR and let you know."
- **Open:** "Good conversation. Get me that one-pager and I'll fight for February."

## SCENE 2: When the small talk dies

- **id:** small-talk-dies
- **Category:** Social | **Difficulty:** medium | **Duration:** 3 min
- **Situation:** You're at your friend Nisha's housewarming, and she's the only person you know. By the snacks, you and a stranger swapped names and jobs. Now there's a silence.
- **Your goal:** Turn this into a real conversation you'd both enjoy.

### Partner
- **Name / age / role:** Sam, 27, UX researcher
- **One-line description:** Dry, curious, a bit shy.
- **Secretly wants:** Sam also only knows Nisha and would be relieved if someone kept talking, but won't carry the conversation alone.
- **Softens when:** You ask something specific | You share something real first | You build on what they said
- **Digs in when:** It feels like an interview | You talk about yourself too long | You change the subject abruptly
- **Verbal habit:** Short answers, bounced back: "Yeah. You?"

### Rubric
| id | Skill | Description | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| curiosity | Asked a real question | Asked for stories, not facts | Swap "what do you do" for "how did you end up doing it." |
| share | Offered something of yourself | Gave an honest detail first | Give a small, honest detail. People match what you offer. |
| build | Built on what they said | Followed their thread | Pick one word from their last answer and ask about it. |
| ease | Kept it light | Lowered the stakes for both | A little self-deprecating humour makes it easier for both of you. |

### Turn 1
- **Guarded:** "Mm. Yeah."
- **Neutral:** "Nice. So... yeah. This dip's good."
- **Open:** "Nice. So... yeah. Honestly, this dip is the best thing here."

**A (strong):** "It is. I've been standing near it on purpose, because I only know Nisha. How do you know her?"
- Reaction: "Ha, same. Only Nisha. We worked together, like, two jobs ago."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: share +1, ease +1 | Trigger: soften, "You share something real first"
- Coach note: Admitting you only know the host is honest, and it gives them an easy way in.

**B (subtle):** "Yeah. So what else do you do, apart from UX research?"
- Reaction: "Uh, not much. Work, sleep. You?"
- Mood: 0 | Skills: build -1 | Trigger: digIn, "It feels like an interview"
- Coach note: It's a question, but another résumé question. It keeps things on the surface.
- Better line: "What's the strangest thing you've learned from watching people use apps?"

**C (backfire):** "Did you see the match last night? Unreal ending."
- Reaction: "I, uh, don't really follow it. Sorry."
- Mood: -1 | Skills: build -1, curiosity -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You change the subject abruptly"
- Coach note: Jumping to something unrelated means Sam has to catch up or admit they can't.
- Better line: "I've been standing near it on purpose, because I only know Nisha. You?"

### Turn 2
- **Guarded:** "Yeah. It's fine. The job's fine."
- **Neutral:** "UX research is basically watching people get confused by apps."
- **Open:** "My job is honestly just watching people get confused by apps and trying not to laugh."

**A (strong):** "Wait, that sounds great. What's the most confused you've seen someone get?"
- Reaction: "Oh, okay. There was this guy who tried to swipe a paper form. Genuinely."
- Mood: +2 | Skills: curiosity +1, build +1 | Trigger: soften, "You build on what they said"
- Coach note: You picked up their exact words and asked for a story, which is easier to answer than a fact.

**B (subtle):** "Oh cool, I'm in product, so I work with researchers a lot. We did like twenty interviews on my last project, and it was really interesting because..."
- Reaction: "Yeah. Interviews. Cool."
- Mood: -1 | Skills: build -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You talk about yourself too long"
- Coach note: Relating is good, but you took the spotlight before they'd finished stepping into it.
- Better line: "Ha, I've done a few interviews myself. What's the most confused you've seen someone get?"

**C (backfire):** "Haha, so you're basically professionally judging people?"
- Reaction: "I mean... no. Not really."
- Mood: -1 | Skills: ease -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Teasing a stranger's job before you know them can land as a jab, not a joke.
- Better line: "That sounds great. What's the most confused you've seen someone get?"

### Turn 3
- **Guarded:** "Anyway. I should probably, um..."
- **Neutral:** "Anyway. What about you? Why are you here if you only know Nisha?"
- **Open:** "Okay, your turn. Why are you here if you only know Nisha? Be honest."

**A (strong):** "Honestly? I almost bailed. I'm glad I didn't, though. This beats my couch."
- Reaction: "Ha. I almost bailed too. Twice."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: share +1, ease +1 | Trigger: soften, "You share something real first"
- Coach note: A small, honest admission invites them to match it, and they did.

**B (subtle):** "Just being supportive, you know. Nisha's a good friend."
- Reaction: "Yeah, she's great."
- Mood: 0 | Skills: share -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: True, but safe. It closes the topic instead of opening one.
- Better line: "Honestly? I almost bailed. I'm glad I didn't."

**C (backfire):** "Parties aren't really my thing. Most of these people seem kind of boring."
- Reaction: "Oh. Well, I'm one of these people, I guess."
- Mood: -2 | Skills: ease -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Honesty is good, but calling the room boring includes the person you're talking to.
- Better line: "Honestly? I almost bailed. This beats my couch, though."

### Turn 4
- **Guarded:** "Yeah. Well. I'm gonna grab a refill."
- **Neutral:** "Okay, this is nice. I'm gonna grab a refill, though. Want anything?"
- **Open:** "Okay, this is the first real conversation I've had tonight. I'm grabbing a refill. Want one?"

**A (strong):** "Yes, please. And then you owe me the rest of the paper-form story."
- Reaction: "Deal. It gets worse, I promise."
- Mood: +1 | Skills: build +1, ease +1 | Trigger: soften, "You build on what they said"
- Coach note: A callback shows you were really listening, and gives them a reason to come back.

**B (subtle):** "No, I'm good, thanks."
- Reaction: "Okay. Nice meeting you."
- Mood: 0 | Skills: build -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Polite, but it lets the conversation end right as it was warming up.
- Better line: "Yes, please. And you owe me the rest of that story."

**C (backfire):** "Sure. Hey, can I get your number, just in case?"
- Reaction: "Oh. Um, I'm on Instagram, I guess?"
- Mood: -1 | Skills: ease -1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Asking for a number mid-refill skips a few steps. Let the conversation finish first.
- Better line: "Yes, please. And then you owe me the rest of that story."

### Endings
- **Guarded:** "Nice meeting you. Enjoy the party."
- **Neutral:** "Nice talking to you. Find me if you need rescuing from small talk."
- **Open:** "Okay, you're sitting with me when Nisha makes everyone play games. That's the rule."

## SCENE 3: Turning down the safe job

- **id:** safe-job
- **Category:** Family | **Difficulty:** medium | **Duration:** 3 min
- **Situation:** You have a stable bank job offer, and your parents have already told relatives. You want to turn it down for a lower-paying startup role in Bengaluru. It's after dinner, and your father has just asked about the joining date.
- **Your goal:** Tell him your decision, and leave him worried but not hurt.

### Partner
- **Name / age / role:** Papa (Rajesh), 54, government employee
- **One-line description:** Proud, practical, protective.
- **Secretly wants:** Reassurance. His nephew Pinku's startup failed badly, and he watched that family struggle. He's proud of you but rarely says it.
- **Softens when:** You thank him first | You show a real plan | You ask what worries him
- **Digs in when:** You say he doesn't understand | You call his thinking outdated | You sound like you haven't planned
- **Verbal habit:** Brings up relatives: "Sharma uncle's son..."

### Rubric
| id | Skill | Description | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| respect | Showed respect | Credited his support and view | Start with what his support made possible, before the news. |
| plan | Showed a real plan | Gave numbers and a fallback | Have three numbers ready: savings, pay difference, and a date to reassess. |
| listen | Listened to the worry | Asked about his fear | Ask "What worries you most?" and let him answer fully. |
| clarity | Stayed clear on the decision | Said it as a decision, warmly | State it as a decision, not a question, while staying warm. |

- Mood: +1 | Skills: respect +1 | Trigger: none
- Coach note: Asking for his view before the news makes him a partner, not an opponent.

- Mood: -2 | Skills: respect -1, listen -1 | Trigger: none

- Mood: -2 | Skills: respect -1, listen -1 | Trigger: digIn, "You call his thinking outdated"

### Endings

- **Neutral:** "Okay. Send me the offer letter. I want to read it myself."
- **Open:** "Okay. I'll tell your mother. And beta... send me the company's name. I want to read about it."
